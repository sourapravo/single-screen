01

Prologue

If you want to reach my home, you have to get down at a bus stop called Parijat. Parijat is not a neighbourhood or a landmark. It is a cinema hall. Or rather, it was.

The cinema hall was demolished over a decade ago. But people still use “Parijat” to give directions to this area. The cinema hall has become a reference point in memory even after its physical disappearance.

The relationship between cinema halls and everyday life is one of the starting points of this project. What remains after a hall disappears is not only an empty site but a network of memories, routes, names, habits and stories.

02

Going to the Movies

Cinema-going was never simply about watching a film. It involved movement through the city, the theatre building, the ticket, waiting, seating, projection and the collective experience of spectators.¹

The cinema hall was therefore both a place of entertainment and a site where different social, technological and spatial arrangements came together.

The experience of going to the movies can therefore be read as a sequence of encounters: street, entrance, ticket counter, lobby, auditorium, screen and projection room.

Technologies of Amazement

Before cinema became a stable institution, moving and projected images emerged through a variety of optical devices and spectacles. Magic lanterns, dissolving views, projection systems and other technologies created experiences that combined machinery, performance and visual wonder.

The technological history of cinema is therefore also a history of amazement: of machines designed to produce images, movement, illusion and a sense of wonder.

04

Cartographies of Cinema in the City

Cinema halls were embedded within the geography of the city. Their locations, streets and neighbourhoods shaped the experience of moviegoing and produced particular relationships between entertainment, mobility and urban space.

The cinema map is therefore not simply a record of locations. It can become a way of reading the changing city through its theatres, streets and audiences.

Cinema as Built Environment

A cinema hall is not simply a container for projection. Its architecture, entrances, seating, projection room, acoustics, electrical systems and relationship to the street all participate in the experience of cinema.

06

Screens in Transition

Single-screen cinemas have been transformed by changes in technology, exhibition, audience behaviour and urban land use. Television, cable, VCD and DVD, digital projection and multiplexes have all altered the conditions of exhibition.
